Greiser's Coffee & Market, 100 years on the corner in Easton, Connecticut

A 100-year-old general store, coffee shop, and gas station in Easton, Connecticut.

Easton, CT

Coffee, market, and vintage gas pump at the corner of Center and Westport. 299 Center Road, Easton, CT 06612

Greiser's sits at the corner of Center and Westport roads in Easton, Connecticut, in a building that has been selling coffee, gasoline and sundries to the town since 1926. An apron on a mannequin inside calls it, with care, "Arguably America's Oldest Coffeehouse." It just celebrated 100 years.

The story

Per Easton Courier (2019), Arthur Richard Greiser immigrated from Germany to Easton in 1900 and bought the store in 1926. Locals called the place "Town Hall West." His son Dick Greiser later ran it as Greiser Market & Deli for 42 years, continuously, selling sandwiches, sundries, gasoline and antiques. The family ran it for 92 years in total.

In November 2018, Adrienne Burke took over the coffee side on a lease from Dick Greiser and reopened the business as Greiser's Coffee & Market. Dick Greiser kept the antiques and the gas station. The coffee operation turned one in 2019 and, in the same building, turned 100 as a continuous Easton coffee destination in 2026.

What's on

Coffee is poured from La Colombe. The espresso menu goes from a $3.50 espresso up to a mocha latte in three sizes, with a cortado, flat white, cappuccino, Americano and macchiato in between. A matcha latte, chai, and hot chocolate round out the drink list. The seasonal panel, the day Glenn visited, carried a minty mocha, a gingerbread-cookie latte and a cinnamon-roll latte. Hot cider is back on. A separate panel lists what the shop calls its "unseasonal lattes," which are rose, strawberry and lavender.

The kitchen side is where regulars will tell you to pay attention. The sandwich list runs to the Greis-n-Shine panini, a sausage, egg and cheese on an English muffin, a Brie & Fig panini, a pickle melt, a brunch burrito and the Groove Cheeseburger. Baked goods include scones, cinnamon buns, savory pastries, a chocolate-chip twist and a daily soup, with gluten-free and vegan options named on the board.

What Glenn thought

Ambiance is the thing here. The coffee operation is a counter at the front of what is still, honestly, a country general store: pressed-tin ceilings, pendant lamps on a pole, antique tables, the mannequin, shelves of pottery and syrup and pickles and lamps. People walk in from the patio where two marble café tables sit between the old gas pumps, and then stay longer than they planned to. Locals pointed out the food as the quiet surprise. The sandwiches and pastries are a bigger reason to stop than the La Colombe pour, which is the kind of thing a visitor doesn't expect from a corner store that is also a gas station that is also a coffee shop.

Before you go

The gas station is temporarily closed, per a handwritten note on the front door. Don't plan on filling up here. Everything else is open to business.

Where to start

Order a latte and a Brie & Fig panini, or whatever the sandwich board is talking up that morning. Sit at a marble table on the patio if the weather allows it. Walk the market on your way out. Then come back for the next season of lattes.
