Coffee shop, Delray Beach, Florida, October 2026

Subculture Coffee in Delray Beach, Florida

A painted former gas station in Delray Beach where the place, the crowd and the food are as much the draw as the coffee.

Delray Beach, FL

Delray Beach shop. 302 NE 6th Avenue, Delray Beach, FL 33444

Subculture's Delray Beach shop is not a coffee geek's destination. The menu board isn't about a carefully curated list of origins. It's a neighborhood meeting place that uses coffee as the draw, and the place is as much a part of the visit as the cup. If you want the opposite approach, a single-origin roaster that is all about the beans, see Dueling Rabbits in Wilmington. This is the other kind of coffee shop, and it's worth knowing too.

The short version

Where
302 NE 6th Avenue, Delray Beach, FL 33444
Part of
Subculture Coffee Roasters, which has six shops in South Florida
Hours
Not posted on the company's site. Check before you go.
Parking
A small lot, which fills up, and street parking on NE 5th and 6th Avenues. The shop posts a map and a request not to park in neighboring businesses' lots.
Order ahead
Online ordering and delivery to your car, per the shop's sign and site

The place

The building is painted from roofline to ground in bold geometric shapes, in red, blue, yellow, orange and black. It used to be a gas station, which the Miami New Times also reports, placing it on Federal Highway.

Inside, Glenn's photos show a steel-trussed ceiling with orange ductwork, stage lights, a motorcycle with bat-like wings mounted on the wall, vintage toy cars and a large painted portrait. Seating ranges from wooden tables and chairs to a built-in bench heaped with patterned cushions, over a painted concrete floor. A pastry case sits at the counter, and an orange pole near the entrance was wrapped in a Halloween skull garland when Glenn visited.

The coffee and the menu

The chalkboard behind the counter listed two drip coffees on the day Glenn visited: a Colombia and one called Cult Classic. The rest of the board is a different kind of menu from a roaster like Dueling Rabbits. It lists syrup flavors including vanilla, caramel, hazelnut, lavender, pistachio, strawberry, rose, coconut, maple and honey, plus sugar-free vanilla and caramel, mocha and white mocha, and spices such as cinnamon, nutmeg and cardamom. A separate board lists hot teas.

Per the company's site, Subculture roasts in small batches in-house and sells locally baked treats, with an emphasis on vegan and homemade cakes. The shop also has plenty of food. For more on the roasting, see the Subculture Coffee Roasters profile.

A digital menu screen near the door, headed "Start," lists the main drinks. Hot drinks run from drip coffee, French press or slow pour, and misto through espresso drinks such as Americano, latte, mocha, cappuccino, flat white, cortado and macchiato, plus tea, chai, matcha and London fog. The cold side has iced coffee, cold brew, iced lattes, iced matcha, affogato and iced tea. The board also lists cold brew cans, singles or six-packs, in black, sweet latte and a THC-infused version marked 21+. A "Fool's Gold" shot of pineapple, lemon, ginger, turmeric and Tellicherry peppercorn and three smoothies (berry, green and breakfast) round it out.

Parking

Parking is the one real drawback. Glenn found the shop's own lot hit or miss, because it has only a few spots and the place is popular. Street parking is plentiful on NE 5th and 6th Avenues, and the shop is within walking distance of locals and visitors, which is part of what keeps it busy.

The shop posts a sign asking customers not to park in any neighboring business's lot, pointing to the street parking on 6th and 5th Avenues, and offering to bring your coffee out to your car if you order online. A hand-drawn map framed near the counter marks the street and lot spaces around the shop and the places not to park, and says 34 spots are available.

The city dispute

The petition sign is not a surprise if you've followed local news. Per CBS12 and WPTV, on March 31, 2026 the Delray Beach City Commission voted 5 to 0 to give owner Rodney Mayo 90 days to present a parking plan. The city says a business of the shop's size needs 44 off-street spaces, and the shop has eight. Two nearby businesses have complained about customers parking in their lots. Commissioners also said the shop has grown from the quick grab-and-go coffee place described when it opened, in 2024 per WPTV, into a venue that hosts events such as poetry nights.

Mayo has said the city is treating the shop unfairly. Per the Miami New Times, a Change.org petition had more than 1,850 signatures at press time, and the city's code enforcement had cited issues including faded parking striping and an unpermitted concrete pad. Mayo told WPTV that the new requirements could push him out of Delray Beach. Per The Palm Beach (August 2026), the city later canceled a vote that could have revoked the shop's parking agreement. The reason for the cancellation wasn't given. We haven't found a report that the shop has closed or moved, but its future in Delray Beach was unsettled at the time of the latest coverage we found.

Before you go

Check the shop's hours and status, since the company's site doesn't post hours and the city dispute is unresolved. Plan to park on the street, or order online and have your coffee brought out. Then go in and look up: the place is as much a part of the visit as the cup.

This profile combines Glenn's visit to the Delray Beach shop and his photos with published sources, researched October 25, 2026. Hours, menus and the shop's situation change, so check before you go. Sources: Subculture Coffee Roasters, CBS12, March 31, 2026, WPTV, March 31, 2026, Miami New Times, and The Palm Beach, August 2026.
