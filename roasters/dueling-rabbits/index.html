Roaster profile, Delaware, October 2026

Dueling Rabbits, the roaster behind a Wilmington bookstore

A one-person roastery and cafe in downtown Wilmington, Delaware, serving coffees from origins most American roasters don't carry.

Wilmington, DE

Roastery and café, in the back of a bookstore. 601 N Market Street, Wilmington, DE 19801

Dueling Rabbits sits at the back of a bookstore on North Market Street in downtown Wilmington. The round shop sign carries two boxing rabbits, an "est 2023," and the shop's motto: "Hop Fast · Sip Slow." On a Saturday morning in October 2026, Glenn stopped in and talked with Adam Rausch, who owns it and roasts every bag himself.

How Adam ended up roasting in Wilmington

Rausch grew up near Cincinnati, spent a year in Lexington, Kentucky, and met his now-wife, who's from Wilmington. He had a candle company then and was roasting coffee on the side as a hobby, giving it out to friends. By the time he and his wife moved to Delaware in 2022, he had already decided the next thing was a roastery and café of his own. He opened Dueling Rabbits in 2023.

Why the menu looks different

Early on, Rausch said, he noticed that when he did events alongside other small roasters, they were all selling the same coffees. The green-coffee marketplaces most roasters buy from stock the same handful of origins: Colombia, Ethiopia, Kenya, Guatemala.

There's over 81 coffee producing regions in the world. Where are the other ones at? They're not on these sites.

Adam Rausch, Dueling Rabbits

So he started contacting farmers himself. Where he can import coffee directly, he does. The result is on the menu board: on the day Glenn visited, the "Rare & Limited" column alone held coffees from Panama, Hawaii, Sri Lanka, Puerto Rico and Trinidad, and the "Uncommon" column added China, Cameroon, Ecuador, Flores, Ghana and the Philippines. Coffees from Puerto Rico and Hawaii grow on United States soil but almost never appear on an American café's menu.

No syrups, by design

You won't find a vanilla latte here. The menu is coffee, and the espresso drinks are the ones a coffee shop served before the big chains came in: doppio, Americano, cortado, cappuccino, flat white, café latte, mocha. The daily drip was $5 on Glenn's visit. A full flight of coffees, for a serious tasting, was $24.

It's a coffee shop for coffee people. Imagine a coffee shop from 100 plus years ago. That's how it is here.

Adam Rausch, Dueling Rabbits

Which is to say: if you want a flavored drink, this isn't the stop. If you want to drink a single-origin pour-over from somewhere you've never heard of, roasted a few feet away, this is exactly the stop.

What Glenn ordered

The Cameroon, as a pour-over. The menu listed it under "Uncommon," from the Grassfields Co-op, with the roaster's tasting notes of toasted coconut and cocoa. $6.00 for a cup. Cameroon rarely appears on an American café menu at all, which is the Dueling Rabbits proposition in a single cup.

Before you go

Seating is small. The shop's own posted rules limit indoor dining to one hour on weekdays and 30 minutes on weekends, no outside food or drinks, and pets or service animals for carry-out orders only. Closed Monday.

Where to start

Ask what Adam has on that doesn't show up on other roasters' shelves. Order a cup of it, and buy a bag to take home if you want to brew it again. If you have time and company, the $24 flight is three cups and the fastest way to taste the range.
