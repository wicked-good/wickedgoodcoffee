Subculture Coffee Roasters in West Palm Beach and Delray Beach, Florida

A South Florida roaster with six shops that each feel different, from its original on Clematis Street to a converted auto site in Delray Beach.

Subculture Coffee Roasters is a South Florida roaster with shops from Boca Raton to Jupiter, built around a simple idea: every location should feel like its own place. Glenn has been to two of them, the original on Clematis Street in West Palm Beach and the one in Delray Beach, and says each has its own feel and size, with an eclectic spirit running through both.

The short version

Where
Six shops, per its site: West Palm Beach (509 Clematis Street), Delray Beach (302 NE 6th Avenue), Boca Raton (437 Plaza Real), Jupiter (107 Front Street), Palm Beach Gardens (11701 Lake Victoria Gardens Avenue) and Lake Worth Beach (522 Lucerne Avenue)
Founders
Restaurateur Rodney Mayo and coffee roaster Sean Scott, per the Broward Palm Beach New Times
Roasting
Small batches, roasted in-house daily, single origins and espresso blends, per its site
Hours
Not posted on the pages we checked. Check the site or call before you go.
Also
Coffee Club subscription, online orders and wholesale, per its site

The place

The company's site says each location has its own design theme, and Glenn's visits bear that out. The West Palm Beach and Delray Beach shops differ in feel and in size, but both come across as eclectic and keen on environmentally friendly messaging. The company's own site says only that its coffee is "ethically sourced," without details, so we can't point to a specific sustainability program behind that impression.

West Palm Beach is the original. Per the New Times, it opened on Clematis Street as a dark, spacious cafe with black-and-white photos of coffee drinkers on the walls and a hand-built wooden coffee bar. Palm Beach Illustrated, in a Best Artisan Coffee award write-up, describes it as an urban gathering place with a roaster displayed at the entrance.

The Delray Beach shop is on NE 6th Avenue, in a building Glenn describes as a former car repair shop.

Per a 2022 OnGardens report, owner Rodney Mayo has said he wants to bring back the 1950s beatnik coffeehouse, a neighborhood place for talking politics. The same report describes the Palm Beach Gardens shop as two stories, with murals inspired by The Man Who Fell to Earth, a brass eyeball and a brass rocket ship.

The coffee

Per its site, Subculture roasts in small batches in-house daily and buys from small-batch farms around the world, with offerings that change through the year because coffee is seasonal. Per the New Times, the original shop uses an American-made Diedrich roaster. Per OnGardens, Mayo said beans rest for three to four days after roasting, are best served about two weeks after roasting, and are thrown out after 30 days. That report is from 2022, so roasting arrangements may have changed.

The shops also serve food and baked goods, with an emphasis on vegan and homemade cakes, per its site.

Recognition

Per Palm Beach Illustrated, Subculture won its Best Artisan Coffee award. The write-up doesn't give a year. A Palm Beach Post reader poll ranked it first for coffee in the Palm Beaches, according to a Waterfront Properties blog post. We haven't seen the poll itself.

Before you go

Hours aren't posted on the company's site, so check with the shop or its site before you go. Glenn notes that parking at the Delray Beach shop is limited.

This profile combines Glenn's visits to the West Palm Beach and Delray Beach shops with published sources, researched October 24, 2026. Hours and menus change, so check before you go. Sources: Subculture Coffee Roasters, Broward Palm Beach New Times, on the West Palm Beach opening, Palm Beach Illustrated, and OnGardens, 2022.
